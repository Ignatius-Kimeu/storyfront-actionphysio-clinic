Treatments

What actually happens in a session.

No two courses look the same, because no two assessments do. But the tools are these four, and the balance between them shifts as you improve.

Hands-on manual therapy

Soft tissue work, joint mobilisation and stretching, done by a physiotherapist rather than a machine. This is usually what settles the pain enough for the rest of the work to be possible. Our patients describe it as “careful assessment” more often than they describe it as treatment — which is rather the point.

Guided exercise & loading

Resistance bands, light weights, the pilates ball, the mat. Movement is prescribed the way a dose is — the right amount, in the right order, progressed when you’re ready. One client came in unable to manage a single bodyweight squat and is now working with 20kg.

In-clinic therapy equipment

Where it helps, treatment is supported by the clinic’s therapy equipment alongside the hands-on work. Your physiotherapist will explain what she’s using and why before she uses it.

The home programme

The part our clients write to us about most. A short, specific set of exercises — not a twenty-page printout — that you can genuinely fit into a day. As one put it: “the physiotherapist has a good work to do, but our part makes it the best for both us.”

Your first session

Mostly listening, before any treating.

Expect the first appointment to be weighted towards assessment. What hurts, how long it’s been going on, what it stops you doing, what you want to get back to. Then testing — movement, strength, the joints above and below the sore one.

Treatment usually starts in the same session. You’ll leave knowing what we think is going on, what we’re going to do about it, and what you’re doing between now and next time.
